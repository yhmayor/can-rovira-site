Since 1863

A house that kept its own name on the bottle

Can Rovira has stayed within the same family since it was bought as a working farm — and its story runs through wine as much as stone.

1863

The estate is acquired

The family bought Mas Monnar, the original farmhouse on the property, and turned the land over to agriculture — chiefly grapevines.

1880s

Wine under its own label

The cellars bottled red wine under the name Alella Can Rovira, grown and pressed on the estate itself.

1888

Gold in Barcelona

That red wine won a gold medal at the 1888 Barcelona Universal Exposition — a result the family still points to.

Today

Still a family house

The rooms keep their original character and the family's own portraits on the walls, now open to guests for stays by the week.

The house

Alella, Maresme coast

Village, vineyards and a short run to Barcelona

Alella sits on the Maresme coast just north of Barcelona, a small town built around narrow old streets and a long history of winemaking — the same hills that once supplied Can Rovira's own cellars still carry vines today, and several of the region's wineries are open for tastings.

The town keeps a slower pace than the city twenty kilometres south, with walking and cycling routes through the surrounding countryside and easy access to the beaches along the coast.
